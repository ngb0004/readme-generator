Middletown, Iowa -- Two controlled detonations are scheduled at Iowa Army Ammunition Plant on Nov. 20 at approximately noon. Residents will be asked to vacate the area from 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. local time, and dust is expected to linger for no more than one hour, depending on wind speed. This is a planned and controlled safety operation to dispose of the degraded lead azide and has been permitted by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency under the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act.

While safety measures will be fully enforced, the controlled detonation will cause temporary noise, ground vibrations, and a dust plume that will contain some lead. Residents in Middletown will hear a loud noise; feel vibrations from two consecutive, rapid detonations; and will see a plume of dust.

Due to the potential for localized window damage, the U.S. Army recommends that residents in the Middletown City Park neighborhood temporarily vacate the area during the detonations out of an abundance of caution. Residents are asked to vacate starting at 8 a.m. when the explosives team begins setting up the detonations. If residents need a place to stay, they are invited to come to the Armed Forces Reserve Center at 16815 DMC HWY 34 Middletown, IA 52638. All family members are welcome, including pets. Please ensure pets are appropriately secure in carrying containers or on leashes. Refreshments will be available at this relatively new building, complete with a gym and classrooms. It is the same location as the open house the Army hosted on the detonation.

The U.S. Army has taken extraordinary measures to mitigate the plume by following strict permit requirements set by the EPA such as earthworks, soil wetting, and air sampling.

Pre-controlled detonation sampling has taken place; air monitors have been established for perimeter air monitoring during the event; and environmental sampling of the blast radius will take place.

MEDIA ADVISORY: You are invited to go to the Armed Forces Reserve Center at 16815 DMC HWY 34 Middletown, IA 52638 to view the detonation.
