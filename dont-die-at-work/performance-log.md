## 001 - Both of These Men Are About to Die (Valero nitrogen, 2005)

- **Incident:** Valero Delaware City refinery, November 5, 2005. Two contract workers asphyxiated by nitrogen.
- **Source:** CSB Case Study 2006-02-I-DE (22 pages).
- **Why this one:** picked from all 134 completed CSB investigations. One invisible cause, a
  would-be-rescuer second act, an absurdly small trigger (a roll of tape), and a lesson that
  applies to anyone who works near a tank, pit or vessel.
- **Format:** Short, 58.5 s, 177 words at 186 wpm.
- **Hook:** "Two men died over this roll of duct tape." Object-first, first frame is the object.
- **Tested variable:** none; this is the baseline everything else is measured against.
- **Results:** pending.

### What to watch when the numbers come in

- Retention at 3 s (does the hook hold?), at ~20 s (the permit beat is the slowest stretch), and at
  ~50 s (do people stay for the tribute?).
- Candidates for the first test, one at a time: a question hook vs this statement hook; 45 s vs 58 s;
  title with the number first ("2 Men, 1 Roll of Duct Tape").

### Review of cut 1 (Nick, 2026-11-21): not approved

What he said, in his words: the animations look "a little scuffed" and need to be "10-fold" better,
"top echelon"; it is "way too fast"; it "doesn't lock me in"; it gets "to the point too quickly"
("duct tape, two guys, they climb into a pit and then they die"); there is "more we can get out of
this situation"; open on how dangerous an ordinary-looking situation is, and play it out.

What the numbers say about "too fast": 177 words in 57 s is 186 words a minute, with the voice sped
up to 1.15x. That was done to fit eight mandatory template sections under the 60 s limit, which
leaves about 7 s a section.

Open questions before cut 2 (Nick's call): the visual style, and whether a Short may run past 60 s.

### More review notes the same day (Nick), which reshaped the channel

In his words:
- "That's not going to stop anybody from doing the exact same thing." The video has to make someone
  in the field understand the danger, the way NTSB accident breakdowns do for him as a student pilot.
- "Where the fuck did the gas come from? What creates the gas?" Explain where the hazard comes from.
- "It's a hook, line, and sinker." A bold claim to stop the scroll, a plain explanation with the
  picture always moving, then the tragedy. "Use humans' emotions."
- "There's 10 guys standing around and nobody is like dying. So nobody thinks that there's any
  danger." Nobody follows every rule every day; he opens live 480 V panels without the full suit.
  The video has to speak from inside that, not from the rulebook.
- His ideas are ideas, not instructions; think big picture and make the calls.

### Cut 2

- **Script:** rebuilt as hook, line and sinker. Opens "Both of these men are about to die." Adds
  the mechanism (why the tank was full of nitrogen, why the body cannot sense it), the days of
  routine that made it feel safe, and what investigators could never establish (whether either man
  knew the purge was on). Closes on "Most shortcuts cost you nothing. That's what makes the one that
  kills you look just like the rest."
- **Sources:** the CSB case study plus the CSB's 2003 nitrogen safety bulletin (for the 80 deaths
  and how nitrogen acts on the body). 73 citations.
- **Look:** diagram style. An engineering cross-section to the report's dimensions, safety-sign
  figures, full-screen cards for the ideas.
- **Length:** 2:02 at 172 words a minute (cut 1: 58.5 s at 186).
- **My own calls, not asked for:** dropped a planned line about the earlier federal warning to make
  room for the "why it felt safe" beat; no wage figure in the hook (the report gives none).
